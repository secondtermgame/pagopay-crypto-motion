# Plastic vs Metal: Which PagoPay Card Should You Choose?

PagoPay offers two card tiers: a **Plastic** card for everyday spending and a **Metal** card for people who want premium perks and a longer-term commitment with no monthly fee. Both cards are built on the same platform, accept the same crypto assets, and work wherever standard card networks are accepted.

This **PagoPay card review** breaks down both tiers side by side so you can decide which fits your lifestyle in 2026.

## The Short Answer

- Choose the **Plastic card** if you want a low upfront commitment, are new to crypto cards, or just want a clean everyday spending tool.
- Choose the **Metal card** if you spend regularly, want lower funding fees, and value perks like concierge support and exclusive events.

For full official details, always check the [Pricing page](/pricing) — pricing and perks can evolve.

## What's the Same on Both Cards

Before the differences, the shared foundation:

- **Same supported assets** — BTC, ETH, USDC, USDT, and more.
- **Same global acceptance** — Wherever standard card networks are accepted.
- **Same core security** — 3DS Secure, card freeze, spending limits, regulated custody.
- **Same conversion engine** — Transparent crypto-to-fiat conversion. For the full breakdown, see [Crypto-to-Fiat Conversion: How It Works and What It Costs](/blog/crypto-to-fiat-conversion-explained).

In other words, the basic act of spending crypto feels identical on both tiers.

## The Plastic Card

The Plastic card is PagoPay's entry-level option. It's the right choice if you want to try a crypto card without a big upfront cost.

Strengths:

- **Low upfront cost** to get started.
- **Standard funding fee** for moving crypto to your card balance.
- **All core features** — accepted globally, supports major assets, full security controls.

Trade-offs:

- A monthly fee applies after the introductory period.
- Funding fee is slightly higher than on the Metal tier.
- No premium perks layer.

If you're brand new to the category, read [What Is a Crypto Card and How Does It Work?](/blog/what-is-a-crypto-card) before deciding.

## The Metal Card

The Metal card is built for people who plan to use their crypto card regularly and want the lowest long-term cost plus a layer of premium perks.

Strengths:

- **No monthly fee** during the included period.
- **Lower funding fee** when moving crypto to your card balance, which adds up if you fund regularly.
- **Premium perks** such as concierge support, exclusive events, and a luxury marketplace.
- **High limits** that support larger purchases without friction.

Trade-offs:

- Higher upfront cost.
- Best value when you actually use it — perks reward active spenders more than occasional ones.

## Choosing Based on How You Actually Spend

Don't decide based on which card looks cooler. Think about your real spending profile.

**Choose Plastic if:**

- You're testing whether a crypto card fits your life.
- Your monthly crypto spend is modest.
- You want to keep upfront costs as small as possible.

**Choose Metal if:**

- You spend on a card most weeks.
- You travel often and want the no-monthly-fee structure plus lower funding fees over a longer horizon.
- You'd actually use perks like concierge support and event access.

If your card mostly handles travel, also read [How to Travel with a Crypto Card in 2026](/blog/crypto-card-travel-guide).

## Stablecoins Make Either Tier Easier to Plan

Regardless of tier, funding with stablecoins like USDC or USDT keeps the spending math simple — $1 in equals roughly $1 to spend, with no volatility between funding and purchase. For more on that pattern, see [Why Stablecoins Are Changing Everyday Spending](/blog/stablecoins-everyday-spending).

## Upgrading or Switching Later

You're not locked in for life. Many users start on Plastic to learn the workflow, then move to Metal once their spending pattern justifies it. The underlying account, supported assets, and spending behavior stay consistent — the upgrade is mainly about cost structure and perks.

For an end-to-end view of using a crypto card day to day, read [How to Spend Crypto in Real Life](/blog/how-to-spend-crypto-in-real-life).

## How to Make the Final Call

A simple 60-second test:

1. Estimate your monthly crypto card spend.
2. Look at the funding fee difference between Plastic and Metal.
3. Add or subtract the monthly fee.
4. Decide whether perks like concierge and events matter to you.

If the math is close and you value perks, Metal wins. If the math favors Plastic and you don't need the perks, Plastic wins. There's no wrong answer — only the one that fits your spending.
